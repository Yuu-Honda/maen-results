    How to read similarity and shared protein counts

    We compare replicate 1 of the selected condition with replicate 2 of another condition, then swap the replicates. When both directions share at least 100 protein IDs, we calculate cosine similarity on the log₂ ratios to the control, then average the two scores. Scores range from −1 to 1. A score closer to 1 indicates a more similar pattern of measured changes. Scores are displayed to three decimal places.

    The shared protein count is shown for each direction. Comparisons use only IDs with values in both measurements. Missing values, measurement groups and shared DMSO controls may influence the scores. Mechanism and treatment effect require separate evidence.

    Counting all 77 possible matches for each of the 78 conditions, 304 of 6,006 pairs (5.1%) came from the same measurement group.

    How to read the chart

    We compare the values measured 12 hours after treatment with the study’s DMSO control. The study shared DMSO controls across measurement groups. Supplementary Table S2 does not specify a unit. Following section 2.5 of the paper, we interpret its values as protein abundance in treated cells divided by abundance in the control. The 1.5 threshold and its reciprocal define the counts. We did not test for statistical significance.

    The chart measures protein abundance in cultured MCF7 cells. To assess whether these changes could be useful for treatment, separate experiments would need to measure cell growth and effects on comparator cells.
